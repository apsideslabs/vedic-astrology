# 1. Foundations

**SOURCE:** chapter I, "The Essentials Explained", and the Introduction.

## The premise

The book opens with a statement of what the system claims to be, and it is
worth quoting closely because everything downstream depends on it.

Hindu astrology is said to rest on **the principle of evolution in time**.
Every cause must produce an action, and the actions of every object are
far-reaching — "in fact, affecting the whole universe". The accumulated
results of a past incarnation are brought into the present; there are moral
principles which must have a long life; every action, physical, moral and
mental, produces in its turn another action. Astrology, on this account,
"simply indicates the results of such actions". The human ego has undergone
many births and has still to pass through many an incarnation before it can
become one with the Supreme Being; in the course of that evolution it seeks
higher or lower forms of terrestrial existence according to the good or bad
deeds (**Karma**) of its previous birth.

Two consequences follow, and the book draws both:

1. **Planets indicate the results of past action** — that is, our future,
   which is divined by astrology. They are indicators, not causes.
2. **Astronomy is the foundation of astrology.** Stars and planets are
   manifestations of matter in space and always obey the law of gravitation;
   they can be located if we know the time. The actual factors employed are
   the planets, the signs, the constellations and other celestial phenomena.

The Introduction adds the physical argument the book uses to make this
plausible: that solar radiation is "undergoing immense modifications every
second", that planetary influence is conveyed by rays and gravitational
waves, and that the moment of birth fixes the individual's relation to those
influences. The argument is of its period and is not evidence; it is recorded
here because it is the book's stated basis.

**CAUTION:** the karma framing is the book's metaphysics, not a finding. The
engine computes positions and applies rules; it does not and cannot test the
premise.

## The zodiac

- The **zodiac** is a broad band in the heavens extending **9 degrees on
  either side of the ecliptic**.
- The **ecliptic** is the Sun's path. It passes exactly through the centre of
  the zodiac longitudinally.
- The zodiac is invisible to the naked eye; it is detected only by watching
  the movements of the planets.
- The ecliptic is divided into **12 equal compartments, the signs**, each
  **30 degrees** in extent.

## The solar system as the book counts it

The solar system is headed by the Sun and consists, including himself, of the
**Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn**. Along with these are
included the shadowy planets **Rahu and Ketu**.

**Uranus, Neptune and Pluto "are considered to have no effect on human
affairs"** — a position the book states flatly and which the engine follows:
the chart has exactly nine grahas.

## The constellations (Nakshatras)

- The ecliptic is marked by **27 constellations** or stellar points.
- They are measured at intervals of **13 degrees 20 minutes** of longitude
  from the first point of Aries, along the ecliptic.
- Each constellation is subdivided into **four quarters (padas)**, each equal
  to **3 degrees 20 minutes** of longitude.
- **According to Hindu astrology, the zodiacal signs and the constellations
  are identical** — both are reckoned from the same point, so the initial
  point of Aries is also the first point of Ashwini.

That last point is the crux of the sidereal system: the nakshatras are fixed
to the stars, and the signs are fixed to the nakshatras, so both are fixed
against precession. See [07-calculation.md](07-calculation.md).

## Naming

Signs are given in Sanskrit and English throughout — Mesha/Aries,
Vrishabha/Taurus, Mithuna/Gemini, Kataka/Cancer, Simha/Leo, Kanya/Virgo,
Thula/Libra, Vrischika/Scorpio, Dhanus/Sagittarius, Makara/Capricorn,
Kumbha/Aquarius, Meena/Pisces.
