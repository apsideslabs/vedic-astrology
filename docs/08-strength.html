The six Shadbalas

"Six sources of strength and weakness (Shadbalas) should be determined for each planet in order to evaluate the worth of a horoscope properly. For the information of the reader, I shall make a passing reference to these sources of strength."

The book then names them and stops, deferring the measurement to its companion volume Graha and Bhava Balas. They are:

1. Sthanabala — positional strength

"The strength acquired due to residence in friendly, own and exaltation signs not only in the zodiacal diagram (Rasi Chakra) but also in the Navamsa and other subdivisions."

So the same dignity test is run twice: once on the Rasi sign, once on the Navamsa sign, and a planet dignified in both (vargottama, when the two signs coincide) is strongest.

2. Digbala — directional strength

"Jupiter and Mercury are powerful in the Ascendant. The Sun and Mars get directional strength in the 10th house. Saturn is powerful in the 7th house, and Venus and the Moon become strong in the 4th house."

Planet Digbala house
Jupiter, Mercury 1st
Sun, Mars 10th
Saturn 7th
Venus, Moon 4th

3. Chestabala — motional strength

"The Sun and the Moon in Capricorn, Aquarius, Pisces, Aries, Taurus and Gemini constituting Uttrayana or summer solstice and Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn in retrogression or conjunction with the Full Moon get Chestabala."

Two conditions, and they are different for the luminaries and for the rest:

  • Sun and Moon: in Capricorn through Gemini (Uttarayana, the northern course).
  • Mars through Saturn: retrograde, or conjunct the Full Moon.

Note: retrogression adds strength here. That is consistent with the Avastha "Sakta or retrogression: courage, wealth, reputation" in chapter I, and it is the opposite of the modern Western instinct.

4. Kalabala — temporal strength

"The Moon, Mars and Saturn are powerful during the night. The Sun, Jupiter and Venus are powerful during the day. Mercury is always powerful. Malefics and benefics are powerful during dark half and bright half of the lunar month respectively. Planets in their weekdays, months and years are said to be powerful."

Condition Strong
night Moon, Mars, Saturn
day Sun, Jupiter, Venus
always Mercury
dark half (Krishna paksha) malefics
bright half (Shukla paksha) benefics
own weekday, month, year that planet

The lunar-month half is the same fact as "the Moon is benefic when waxing, malefic when waning" from chapter I, seen from the strength side.

5. Drukbala — aspect strength

"Aspects of benefics give full Drukbala while aspects of malefics take away Drukbala."

The engine already records every aspect with its sign distance and sight fraction; Drukbala is the sum over incoming aspects, signed by the aspecting planet's nature.

6. Saisargikabala — natural strength

"Each planet is supposed to produce a particular measure of strength permanently irrespective of its position. The Sun, Moon, Venus, Jupiter, Mercury, Mars and Saturn are strong in descending order. The Sun is the most powerful and Saturn is the least powerful."

So the fixed order is Sun > Moon > Venus > Jupiter > Mercury > Mars > Saturn.

What this engine actually computes

This is important and is stated plainly in the API too. The engine computes a simplified positional strength, not Shadbala. It is an additive score with every component reported separately, so a reading can be audited:

Component Value
dignity (exalted … debilitated) +2.0 … −2.0
house kind — kendra / panapara / apoklima +0.75 / 0 / −0.5
trine (1, 5, 9) +0.5
Digbala (in its directional house) +0.5
Chestabala (retrograde) +0.25
combustion −0.75
vargottama (Navamsa sign = Rasi sign) +0.5

The raw total is mapped onto 0–100 for display only. What is not implemented: the full Sthanabala sub-divisions (Saptavargaja etc.), the Kala/Digbala hour-and-minute measures, Drukbala as a numeric sum, and Saisargikabala's calibrated units. Anyone who needs Shadbala should use a dedicated library, or port Graha and Bhava Balas.

The judgement rules of chapter III

These are the rules the book gives for actually using strength, and they are more valuable than the Shadbala list:

"The lords of trines are always auspicious and produce good. When benefics own quadrants, they produce evil. When malefics own quadrants they produce good."

"The good and evil planets become more and more powerful as they are lords of the 1st, the 4th, the 7th or the 10th houses."

"Lords of 3, 6, 8 and 11 do evil and cause miseries."

"The lords of the 2nd and the 12th houses give good results if they are in conjunction with favourable planets."

"The lord of the 8th, if he chances to be the lord of the 1st or is in conjunction with a benefic, becomes good."

"Rahu and Ketu produce good results when they are posited in the sign of the benefic planets."

"Jupiter and Venus owning quadrants become very inauspicious. Mercury as the lord of a quadrant is less malefic than Jupiter and Venus, and the Moon lesser than Mercury. Mars does not become good when he owns only the 10th house. He must own the 5th also to become thoroughly good."

"If the lords of Kendras (quadrants) without being associated with the lords of other houses, they become powerful in producing good results."

That last sequence is the classical Kendradhipati dosha — the blemish of owning an angle — and it is why a benefic ruling a quadrant turns malefic. It also explains the Yogakaraka: a planet that owns both a quadrant and a trine escapes the dosha and becomes the best planet in the chart. The textbook example is Saturn for Taurus (9th and 10th) and for Libra (4th and 5th); the engine derives yogakarakas from ownership rather than from a table, so the answer falls out of the Ascendant.

The book's caution about judgement itself

"The judgment of a horoscope forms the most important and difficult task of an astrologer. It must be borne in mind that, at the outset no hard and fast rules can be laid down for the guidance of the reader. In order to examine a horoscope, good power of judgment is required."

"Unskilful manipulation of co-ordinates employed may lead to erroneous conclusions."

Any software that presents a reading as a verdict is working against the book's own instruction.
