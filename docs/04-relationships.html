4. Relations between planets

SOURCE: chapter I, "Relations between Planets".

The book says the subject is dealt with fully in Raman's Hindu Predictive Astrology, and gives the outline here. Relations are of two kinds, permanent and temporary.

"A planet is said to be a friend of the other whose rays it does not counteract."

Permanent (natural) friendship

The book prints the table on pp. 7–8. The scan is badly damaged across the page break: the rows for the Sun, Moon, Mars and Mercury are cut through, and the Venus, Jupiter and Saturn rows are reordered by the OCR. The table below is the standard Parashari table, which is what the book is presenting; the fragments that survive in the scan are consistent with it.

Planet Friends Neutral Enemies
Sun Moon, Mars, Jupiter Mercury Venus, Saturn
Moon Sun, Mercury Mars, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn —
Mars Sun, Moon, Jupiter Venus, Saturn Mercury
Mercury Sun, Venus Mars, Jupiter, Saturn Moon
Jupiter Sun, Moon, Mars Saturn Mercury, Venus
Venus Mercury, Saturn Mars, Jupiter Sun, Moon
Saturn Mercury, Venus Jupiter Sun, Moon, Mars
Rahu Venus, Saturn, Mercury — Sun, Moon, Mars
Ketu Mars, Venus, Saturn — Sun, Moon

STANDARD: the Rahu and Ketu rows are not in the book at all — it deals with the seven grahas. The values above are the common convention.

Temporary friendship

SOURCE: chapter I, verbatim:

"Planets found in the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 10th, 11th and 12th signs from any other planet become the latter's temporary friends. The others are its enemies."

So temporary friendship is a function of sign position only:

Distance from the planet Temporary relation
2, 3, 4, 10, 11, 12 friend
5, 6, 7, 8, 9 enemy
same sign own

The book's worked illustration: with Jupiter in the 2nd from the Sun, Venus in the 12th, and the Moon and Mars in the 3rd, "Jupiter, Venus, Moon, Mars and Mercury are the Sun's temporary friends. Saturn alone is the Sun's enemy, temporarily."

Mixed relations

SOURCE: chapter I, "Mixed Relations" — "In order to find the strengths of planets we have to mix the temporary relations and the permanent relations. Thus a temporary enemy plus a permanent or natural enemy becomes a bitter enemy."

Natural relation Temporary friend Temporary enemy
Friend intimate friend friend
Neutral friend enemy
Enemy neutral bitter enemy

That is the standard combination table and it is what the engine implements in strength.compound_relation.

The book's caution, which matters more than the table:

"It is to be understood that a temporary friendship will not entirely overcome a radical and constitutional enmity."

Relations between planets and signs

SOURCE: chapter I. Four kinds of relationship are of particular importance: ownership, exaltation, debilitation and moolatrikona. All four are tabulated in 03-planets.md.

The engine ranks them in the order the book implies — exaltation outranks moolatrikona ("the power to do good when in exaltation is greater than when in its own sign"), which outranks ownership, which outranks friendship:

exalted  >  moolatrikona  >  own  >  intimate friend  >  friend
         >  neutral  >  enemy  >  bitter enemy  >  debilitated

Why relations matter

Everything downstream uses them. The book uses friendship to:

  • decide whether a planet is "rendered powerful to do good" in a sign (chapter I);
  • decide the quality of a dasha, since "the relations between the lords of the main period and sub-period should be considered when predicting events during their time" (chapter X);
  • decide longevity, by asking whether the lord of the Lagna and the lord of the 8th from the Moon are friendly, inimical or neutral (chapter IV);
  • decide whether an aspect is good or bad, since "an aspect is good or bad according to the relation between the aspecting and the aspected body" (chapter I).
