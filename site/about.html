Provenance and limits

What this is built from, what was damaged in the source, and what it therefore cannot do.

The source

B. V. Raman, Astrology for Beginners — 135 pages, twelve chapters and two appendices, published by UBS Publishers' Distributors, New Delhi. The copy this was extracted from is a scan held by the Internet Archive, recoded by LuraDocument in 2017, carrying an OCR text layer of about 147,000 characters. Pages 1, 6, 8 and 10 are near-empty (cover plate and blank versos), and pages 60–63 are duplicated in the scan itself.

What was done to it

  1. The text layer was extracted page by page.
  2. The running watermark was stripped and the one-word-per-line OCR layout reflowed into paragraphs.
  3. Chapters were segmented by page range and read in full.
  4. The content was reorganised by subject rather than by chapter, because the book returns to the same topics repeatedly.
  5. Every table was transcribed by hand from the reflowed text.

Nothing was paraphrased into a claim the book does not make. Where the book is wrong or dated, it is marked as a caution rather than silently corrected.

What the book has and this does not

  • Ashtakavarga — chapter XII refers to a separate volume. Not implemented.
  • Full Shadbala — chapter III refers to Graha and Bhava Balas. What is implemented is a transparent additive positional score, labelled as such everywhere it appears.
  • Varshaphala, muhurta, electional astrology — other books in the series.
  • Divisional charts beyond D9 — the book treats only the Navamsa.
  • Compatibility matching — not in the book at all.

Two rules documented but deliberately not implemented

The navamsa count method for the number of brothers and children, and the Sade Sati first / second / third passage reading (“during the third time the death of the native is invariably caused”). Both are recorded in the knowledge base with their cautions. They are the two rules in this book most likely to be read as a prediction about a real family or a real death, and no software should assert either.

Verification

The book works a chart out by hand in chapter II and the engine reproduces it — nine grahas within a few arcminutes, the Rasi chart, the dignities, the Mars–Jupiter exchange, and the Vimshottari balance of 6 years 1 month 15 days. Thirty-four tests assert this. If a transcription error had crept into the exaltation table, the ayanamsa, the nakshatra spans, the navamsa formula or the dasha arithmetic, those tests would fail.

The browser build is checked the same way: tools/verify_js.js and tools/verify_py.py dump the same chart from both implementations and compare them field by field. They currently agree exactly — positions, houses, dignities, dasha balance, panchanga, yogas, aspects, transits and the structure of the reading.

What this is for

Study and self-reflection. Not medical, financial or legal advice, and not a factual prediction about any person. The book says as much itself:

“The judgment of a horoscope forms the most important and difficult task of an astrologer. It must be borne in mind that, at the outset no hard and fast rules can be laid down for the guidance of the reader.”
